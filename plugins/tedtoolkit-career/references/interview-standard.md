# Interview Standard

Use these shared rules for candidate preparation and interviewer design. Apply the separate output
and audience rules from the owning skill.

## Coverage

- Allocate time and question volume by the role's highest-value responsibilities, required
  qualifications, domain constraints, and material evidence gaps.
- Balance past behavior, technical or domain depth, situational judgment, contribution ownership,
  and role motivation when relevant.
- Avoid trivia, riddles, duplicate questions, leading questions, and generic prompts that do not
  affect readiness for the role.
- With both candidate evidence and a job description, use `match-job-description` as supporting
  analysis. With only one source, state the corresponding limitation instead of inventing the other.

## Question quality

Every primary question must identify the competency or source requirement it covers and include
neutral follow-up probes. State what concrete evidence a useful answer should contain. Include at
least one probe that separates individual contribution from a collective result when a source claim
describes team work.

Questions must test or rehearse evidence, decisions, constraints, trade-offs, and outcomes. Do not
assume that an unmentioned capability is absent; use the meaning `Not demonstrated` from
`career-integrity.md`.

## Timeboxing

Allow enough time for complete answers and follow-up probes. When requested question volume cannot
fit the timebox, extend the plan transparently or mark excess questions as alternates rather than
pretending they all fit.

## Fairness

Keep every interviewer question job-related. Apply the protected-trait exclusions and legal-source
boundary in `career-integrity.md`. Candidate preparation may explain how to evidence a legitimate
requirement, but must not encourage disclosure of irrelevant sensitive information.
