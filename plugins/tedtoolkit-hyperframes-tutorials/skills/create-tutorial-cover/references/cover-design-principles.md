# Cover Design Principles

Use this reference when creating, comparing, reviewing, or revising a course or lesson cover. It
turns general graphic-design guidance into decisions that are useful for a course-cover family. It
does not replace the course's established brand, visual mode, or verified lesson evidence.

## Start from the communication job

Before composing, state the cover's one primary communication job in a sentence:

- a course cover promises the course's observable outcome and recognizable subject;
- a lesson cover identifies this lesson and gives one truthful reason to distinguish it from the
  rest of the series.

Identify the intended viewer and discovery context. A familiar visual cue may help an existing
learner recognize the series, while a new learner needs a clear subject and outcome. Do not solve
for curiosity by weakening accuracy. The cover and adjacent publication title should tell one
compatible story rather than repeat every word or make conflicting promises.

Reduce the candidate content to three roles before laying it out:

1. **Primary:** the title or focal teaching subject that must be understood first.
2. **Secondary:** the complementary title or focal visual that explains the primary signal.
3. **Tertiary:** a compact course mark, chapter badge, or other identity cue.

Remove an element that has no distinct job. A cover is not a miniature lesson slide, table of
contents, or dashboard.

## Establish hierarchy, not merely decoration

Guide the eye in a deliberate order. Create hierarchy through a combination of scale, position,
contrast, weight, and negative space; do not make every element loud. Use one dominant focal area
and normally no more than two supporting groups. Two or three visibly distinct type or object scales
are usually enough to communicate rank.

Use Gestalt grouping deliberately:

- **Proximity:** keep a title, badge, and related label close only when they form one information
  group. Increase space between unrelated roles.
- **Similarity:** repeat shape, color role, typography, or framing to identify the course family.
  Reserve a controlled difference for the lesson-specific subject.
- **Common region:** use a panel or boundary only when it clarifies grouping. Too many containers
  turn the cover into interface chrome.

Run a first-glance test at thumbnail size: the intended primary element must be the first defensible
answer to "what did you notice first?" If title, badge, illustration, and decoration compete, reduce
or remove signals instead of adding another effect.

## Build a reproducible composition

Define a grid or repeatable key lines before placing details. Align the title block, focal visual,
course mark, and chapter badge to a small set of horizontal and vertical anchors. Use a consistent
spacing unit or scale to create rhythm; the exact unit may follow an existing brand system. Do not
force every object onto the grid when optical alignment produces the clearer result.

Balance visual weight rather than mirroring geometry. Asymmetry is valid when the title block and
focal visual counterbalance each other. Check the composition by squinting or temporarily blurring
it: large masses, contrast, and empty space should still feel intentional, and one side should not
collapse under several small high-contrast details.

Keep useful negative space around the title and focal subject. Empty space separates roles and gives
future crop variants room; it is not unfinished background that must be filled.

## Set title typography for thumbnail reading

Use the real course or lesson title, or a truthful display headline approved through the cover
concept. Line-break it at semantic phrase boundaries; do not silently rewrite, abbreviate, or add a
marketing claim merely to make the layout easier. When the official title is too long for a legible
cover, propose a separate display headline while preserving the official identity in the course
records.

Choose typefaces that contain every required glyph and punctuation mark in the course language.
Prefer the established course family. Add a second family only when it has a clear contrast role and
remains coherent with the brand. Avoid thin weights, narrow counters, excessive tracking, ornate
display faces, and long all-caps Latin text at thumbnail size.

For Chinese and mixed Chinese-Latin titles:

- keep Chinese line breaks semantically meaningful;
- do not leave closing punctuation at the start of a line or opening punctuation at the end;
- do not split tokens such as `pH`, `ISO 400`, version numbers, or short identifiers;
- check the optical baseline and weight of Han characters, Latin letters, numerals, and symbols;
- avoid arbitrary expansion of Chinese character spacing to fill a box.

Render the actual title early. A typographic system is not verified by a placeholder whose length,
script, or punctuation differs from the final title.

## Use a low-copy Bilibili profile

For a Bilibili publication cover, assume the image will compete in a dense mobile information feed
and that the full video title is visible nearby. The cover should add a fast visual signal, not become
a second title card.

Use these as working design heuristics rather than platform upload rules:

- when text is useful, prefer one display headline of `4–10` Han characters and normally cap it at
  `15` full-width-character equivalents; a text-free cover is valid when one strong visual and the
  adjacent publication title already do the communication job;
- keep it to one or two lines and one to three semantic chunks;
- use a bold or heavy face, strong local contrast, and a headline large enough to retain roughly
  `28–40` pixels of Han-character height when the whole cover is reduced to 320 pixels wide;
- omit subtitles, descriptions, lesson numbers, software-version strings, and course branding when
  they do not survive reduction or perform a distinct recognition job;
- use one close, recognizable teaching subject or result rather than several small screenshots;
- use the adjacent publication title for precision and the cover headline for rapid recognition.

The character range is deliberately a starting constraint, not a reason to damage meaning. A short
technical token such as `pH`, `ISO 400`, or `RAW` may count as one semantic unit. If a truthful headline needs
more words, rework the phrase or composition and verify it at thumbnail size; do not automatically
shrink the type. An established series mark may stay small as a recognition cue, but no required
information should depend on reading it.

Inspect Bilibili derivatives at both 320 and 160 pixels wide. The 160-pixel version is a stress test,
not a second master: the headline and primary subject must still register at a glance. When it fails,
reduce copy before adding outlines, shadows, glow, or more saturated decoration.

## Assign color by role

Start with the established course palette. Give colors stable jobs such as background, primary text,
secondary text, course accent, and chapter accent. Prefer one dominant base, one purposeful accent,
and necessary neutrals over several equally saturated colors.

Use contrast to establish hierarchy and legibility. As a measurable floor, test informative text and
images of text against their immediate background using WCAG's 4.5:1 threshold for ordinary text
and 3:1 for large display text. These web thresholds are a conservative diagnostic, not proof that a
cover works: reduction, textured imagery, compression, thin strokes, and local background changes
can still make nominally passing text unreadable. When text sits over an image, verify the darkest
and lightest relevant regions or introduce a controlled solid panel, gradient, or scrim.

Do not rely on hue alone to distinguish a chapter or lesson. Preserve a shape, label, number, or
position cue, and inspect a grayscale version to reveal weak hierarchy and indistinguishable accents.

## Choose imagery that carries evidence

Prefer one focal subject or one coherent visual relationship over a collage. A course cover may
synthesize the approved course promise. A lesson cover must remain grounded in its verified video;
recomposition may simplify a frame but must not fabricate an interface, result, capability, or
emotion absent from the lesson.

Crop deliberately. Preserve the subject's meaningful action, direction, and visual breathing room.
Avoid decorative stock imagery or generated symbols that introduce a second metaphor unrelated to
the teaching subject. Treat a logo or familiar product mark as identity, not as the main teaching
evidence unless the lesson is actually about that product.

If the background is detailed, reduce its competition through crop, depth, value, saturation, or a
local overlay. Do not solve every conflict with heavy outlines, glow, drop shadows, or multiple
effects around the title.

## Design a family with controlled variation

Make the course family recognizable by fixing a small visual grammar:

- course mark position and scale;
- title grid and alignment;
- typography roles;
- recurring frame, shape, or image treatment;
- base palette and chapter-accent logic;
- outer safe-area policy.

Vary the lesson title, chapter marker, focal evidence, and one controlled accent. Similarity should
identify the series, while the focal evidence and title prevent individual covers from becoming
interchangeable. Compare covers in a contact sheet; a single attractive cover can still fail the
family when its fixed elements drift or every lesson uses the same subject.

## Design for reduction and cropping

Judge the canonical cover at full size and at 320 pixels wide. At the reduced size, verify in this
order:

1. the course or lesson remains identifiable;
2. the main title can be read without zooming;
3. the focal subject remains distinct from the background;
4. the course mark is recognizable but does not compete with the title;
5. decorative details may disappear without changing the meaning.

Preview every requested destination crop with an explicit mask. Keep essential title text, identity,
faces, and focal actions inside the intersection of required safe areas. Allow nonessential texture
or background to extend into crop-loss zones. Do not shrink all content merely to survive arbitrary
future crops; support only the canonical aspect ratio and named destination profiles.

## Compare concepts by structural difference

When direction is open, make two or three concepts that differ in information structure, for
example:

- title-led editorial composition;
- focal-diagram or teaching-object composition;
- evidence-frame composition grounded in a representative lesson visual.

Changing only background color, icon choice, or decoration does not create a meaningful concept.
Give each concept a short rationale naming its focal order, promise, asset source, and principal
tradeoff. Do not label one concept as preferred until it has passed the checks below.

## Review in passes

Review a candidate in this order so polish does not hide a structural failure:

1. **Truth:** title, subject, outcome, and imagery match the approved course or verified lesson.
2. **Hierarchy:** one primary signal wins; secondary and tertiary roles are clear.
3. **Typography:** final-language glyphs, line breaks, punctuation, weight, and mixed-script spacing
   are correct.
4. **Composition:** key lines, grouping, negative space, balance, and crop room are intentional.
5. **Color:** contrast is measurable, accents have stable roles, and grayscale still communicates
   hierarchy.
6. **Reduction:** the 320-pixel thumbnail preserves identity, title, and focal clarity.
7. **Destinations:** every requested crop mask preserves essential content.
8. **Family:** a contact sheet shows both continuity and enough lesson differentiation.

Reject or revise a candidate when any earlier pass fails. Do not compensate for weak hierarchy or
truth with texture, glow, gradients, or additional badges.

## Common failure patterns

- equal visual weight for title, badge, logo, illustration, and metadata;
- long subtitles, dense code, or paragraph-like text;
- a busy screenshot used as an unmodified background;
- thin text, low local contrast, or type that works only at full resolution;
- arbitrary line breaks that damage Chinese phrasing or strand punctuation;
- unrelated metaphors, stock imagery, or generated spectacle;
- platform-specific crops silently replacing the canonical course master;
- every lesson cover repeating the same screenshot or illustration;
- decorative interface chrome that makes the cover look like a dashboard;
- clickbait emotion or results unsupported by the course or lesson.

## Source basis

These sources support the principles above; revisit current platform documentation when an export
depends on a platform-specific requirement.

- [Nielsen Norman Group: Visual Design Principles](https://media.nngroup.com/media/articles/attachments/Principles_Visual_Design-A4.pdf) — scale, hierarchy, balance, contrast, and Gestalt grouping.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: Proximity](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-proximity/),
  [Similarity](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/gestalt-similarity/), and
  [Common Region](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/common-region/) — perceived relationships and
  grouping.
- [IBM Carbon Design System: 2x Grid](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/2x-grid/overview) — key lines, spacing rhythm, alignment, and aspect-ratio structure.
- [W3C WCAG 2.2 contrast criteria](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag/#contrast-minimum) — measurable
  text and image-of-text contrast thresholds.
- [W3C Requirements for Chinese Text Layout](https://www.w3.org/TR/clreq/) — Chinese punctuation,
  line breaking, mixed-script layout, and regional typography considerations.
- [YouTube thumbnail and title guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300) —
  audience fit, readable type, controlled complexity, branding, accuracy, and post-publication
  evaluation.
- [Bilibili: 小柴哥工具箱——录屏、封面图、文案小 Tips](https://www.bilibili.com/opus/534928959721735961)
  — emphasizes adapting covers to multiple thumbnail forms, a close recognizable subject, and a
  simple combination of text and visual elements.
- [Bilibili: 投稿贴士与问题反馈](https://www.bilibili.com/blackboard/activity-zBCxZcBxY8.html)
  — requires a clear cover that matches and expresses the video's subject; verify current upload
  dimensions in the active publishing interface rather than treating this older page as canonical.
- [Bilibili creator example: 知识区“大字报”封面制作法](https://www.bilibili.com/video/BV1qJzrYUExL/)
  — a practical example of the low-copy pattern: a short headline, one to three emphasized keywords,
  heavy type, and high contrast. Its `15`-character suggestion informs the working ceiling above; it
  is not a Bilibili platform requirement.
